Wikipedia for files

Arca is a shared library that people everywhere build together. Anyone can add files, anyone can search and read them, and the people who keep them safe are paid for it. No servers: every app is a relay, every byte travels over I2P, and a phone is a full participant.

Files vanish

Radio archives, maps, books, recordings, manuals, family photos and community records live on single servers and personal drives. They disappear when one person stops paying or one service shuts down. Arca gives files what Wikipedia gave articles.

One place to add themAnyone joins a circle and proposes files for its collections. Contributing is free.
One way to find themEvery circle publishes a catalog and a search index anyone can verify by recomputing a sample.
Many hands keeping themPeople keep copies of the collections they care about, and are paid for keeping what others also want kept.

Six ideas at the heart of Arca

Each one exists somewhere on its own. Arca is the first to put them together for files.

1

Proof of Keeping

Mining is keeping files people chose to keep. Keepers prove every day that they still hold their copies and earn marcas for it. The more meaningful data people keep, the stronger the network.

2

Circles that curate, like Wikipedia

Communities build collections together: anyone proposes, moderators accept, every version is signed. Members who dislike their moderators fork the circle with their disks the same day.

3

Every client is a relay

Identities, comments, likes and edits are Nostr events. There are no relay servers: every Arca app stores and answers them itself, over I2P, so no server ever learns an IP address.

4

Phones are full participants

The chain keeps state, not history. A phone follows headers, reads its balance with Merkle proofs and drops any block an honest node proves wrong with a few-KB fraud proof.

5

Private money, public keeping

Payments hide amounts, payer and payee (Mimblewimble with stealth outputs). What earns stays public and auditable: anyone can check that no marca was ever made from nothing.

6

Files that carry their knowledge

Each file's description and subtitles sit next to it as Talk.arca.json and Talk.en.srt. Subtitles are made on the device by whisper.cpp, so speech becomes searchable.

Proof of Keeping

One coin, the marca, created only by the global chain on a fixed schedule. Its mining input is the keeping of data someone chose to keep. The rules are built so that faking it always costs more than doing it.

The replication curve

Rewards for a piece of data rise with each keeper up to ten copies, then freeze and are split among all. The incentive: be one of the first ten keepers of something worth keeping.

12345678910111213

Total reward of a piece of data by number of copies (n squared over 100 of ten copies' worth, frozen from the eleventh).

Two budgets each day

Storage 30%
Interest 70%
  • Storage pays for bytes kept, deduplicated, so even rarely read archives are kept.
  • Interest pays for what the wider library wants: keepers from other circles and paid reading, never free clicks.
  • Half of every pass is burned, so buying your own popularity always loses half.
  • Only a third of standing passes on, so rings of circles keeping each other's junk are capped at 1.5x honest earnings.

Every copy is unique

Each chunk is stored XOR a memory-hard keystream of the keeper's key. Remaking a slice costs 1,355x reading it (measured, testnet), so one disk cannot pretend to be a hundred.

Speed buys nothing

A clock names random slices once a second; a faster disk reads no more of them. A Raspberry Pi with used disks competes.

Caught within a day

Each day names one random chunk per declared partition; a missed or wrong 2 KB proof drops everything the keeper had built up.

Private by construction

Arca publishes what must be checked to be rewarded, and nothing that maps a person or a machine to a place or a time.

I2P only

No clearnet path, no relay on the internet, no server that learns an address. The pure Dart I2P node runs inside the app.

Separate profiles

Each profile has its own key and I2P address; nothing links two profiles unless their owner does.

Nothing that locates you

Latency, throughput and serving limits never leave the device. Search history and opened files stay local.

Private payments

Pedersen commitments, Bulletproof range proofs and stealth outputs: amounts, payers and payees hidden; supply still auditable by anyone.

What works today

A working prototype for Linux and Android, tested on the live I2P network and on a public test network of its own chain. Test marcas have no value.

Get started

Build the app, open it, and you have a profile, an I2P address and a library. Join the test network from the wallet with one button.

git clone --recursive https://github.com/x1watt/arca
git clone https://github.com/x1watt/i2p-dart
cd arca/app
flutter build linux        # or: flutter build apk

Then

  • Create a collection from a folder and describe your files.
  • Share your address so others follow, comment and suggest edits.
  • Keep a copy of a collection you care about.
  • Download a speech model for subtitles in Settings.
  • In the wallet: "Take part in the test network".
